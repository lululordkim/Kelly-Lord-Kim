The Practice of Separation

On choosing what we cultivate.

My teacher once said something that illuminated my moment-to-moment practice. She said, "We all have a lifestyle — it's either a lifestyle of reification or a lifestyle of awareness."

I felt the truth of that statement immediately. It wasn't philosophy; it was a pointer to a subtle tension I was holding without knowing it: I was never not practicing. Every moment was either the cultivation of separation or resting as awareness. We are never neutral in a certain sense. We're always choosing, always creating.

After this pointing, I could feel when I'd slip back into being someone trying to manage it all. I saw how easily attention collapsed into the fiction of a "me" who was managing the process, and how if I practiced this fiction, it would become a lifestyle.

Distraction is a practice. Hurry is a practice. Worry, planning, resistance — each one trains the mind to believe in separation. Every moment of indulgence in busyness repeats the same gesture: I am separate and apart.

The Cultivation of Separation

Within awareness, there is a quiet, continuous movement we've been conditioned to sustain — the cultivation of a lifestyle of separation. It shows up in the reflex to label, in the subtle resistance to what's already here, in the grasping for clarity as though clarity weren't the very substance of this moment.

This isn't wrong. It's simply the practice we inherited. And while this training constructs a coherent life story and gets things done, its success is limited to the perception of the separate self. Restlessness is its trace — an undercurrent of incompletion, as if peace exists just out of reach.

The Choice to Soften

Her words shifted something: I saw I wasn't a person practicing awareness. Awareness was already here, either resting as itself or momentarily identifying as the one who seeks. I heard her words from within that agency — the freedom to notice and let be.

From there, I could stop reinforcing separation. And in that stopping, a different kind of practice began — less doing, more allowing. I could let the world appear exactly as it does, let this person with this history unfold naturally, and yet hold no certainty that this person is the entirety of who and what I am.

At times, the activity of practicing subsides. The awareness that's reading these words right now doesn't need to be cultivated or improved. There is nothing to manage or maintain.

What remains is ordinary and complete — what's always been seeing, thinking, breathing.

The noticing itself is the shift.

The sky was never separate from blue. We are never separate from this.
