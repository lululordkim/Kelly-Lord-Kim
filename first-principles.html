First Principles of
Nonduality

Strip away assumption until you hit bedrock. Five propositions that all traditions converge on.

Most nondual books either presuppose a tradition — you need to trust Ramana, or trust the Course — or presuppose the seeking. This project applies first-principles reasoning to the inner life. Not devotionally. Not academically. As direct inquiry.

When you lay Advaita Vedanta, Dzogchen, and A Course in Miracles alongside each other, five bedrock propositions emerge that all three affirm.

One — Awareness Is Prior to Everything

Not prior in time. Prior in nature. Awareness is not a product of the brain — the ground in which all experience arises. Advaita calls this Brahman, self-luminous. Dzogchen calls it rigpa, already present, never absent. ACIM calls it the one Self unified with its Creator.

Two — Separation Is Not Real, But It Is Believed

The felt sense of being a separate self is a perceptual distortion, not ontological fact. This is separation bias — and all three traditions name it as the root of suffering. You can only ever feel or think you are separate. You can never, ever be separate.

Three — What You Seek, You Already Are

Not attainment. Recognition. The seeker and the sought are the same movement. This is the most radical convergence across traditions — and the hardest for the Western mind to receive, because the Western mind is organized around acquisition. See Seeking Wisely on the confusion of correlation with causation in spiritual seeking.

Four — The Via Negativa Is Not Enough

Removing false belief clears the ground. Essential. But incomplete. Something must be recognized as that ground. All three traditions point to what IS — not only what is not. Without this second movement, students land in no-man's-land. This is explored in depth in Beyond the Lens of "Me".

Five — Practice Is Recognition, Not Construction

You are not building anything. You are noticing what was never absent. The ground is already present. Always has been. Brother Lawrence in the kitchen — not achieving presence, but recognizing that presence is always already here. See Practicing the Presence and The Practice.
