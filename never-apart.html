Never Apart

On longing, love, and the wholeness already here.

There is a longing that moves through us, seeking completion.

That longing isn't separate from what it seeks. The impulse to find peace, happiness, wholeness is already the wholeness appearing as search.

Even the wanting is made of what is wanted. The energy that moves toward fulfillment is fulfillment itself, expressing as movement. What we call "seeking" is love looking for itself in every form.

When an experience comes and goes, what remains untouched? What is aware of both the satisfaction and its fading?

This awareness — never empty, never full, never seeking — is what every search points toward.

What longs is what loves.

The restlessness that seems to empty us is the very fullness appearing as movement. Even when we receive what we thought we wanted, the heart that "can't seem to stay full" isn't broken — it's recognizing its own infinite nature, too vast to be contained by any experience.

The beloved who promises forever, the work that calls forth our gifts, even our children who once seemed to complete us — none of these are false. They reveal what's always true: that love seeks to know itself everywhere, as everything.

What believes it can be completed is the same presence that appears as incompletion.

Maybe we were never meant to seek completion at all. Maybe we were meant to recognize it — to see there was never anything missing.

Time is the dream of a mind that believes completion lies elsewhere. But what we are is prior to time — the aware presence in which all experience appears and dissolves.

The peace we seem to be searching for is the same peace that's doing the searching. It isn't a feeling that comes and goes; it's the awareness that quietly knows every feeling.

Happiness isn't found at any peak. It's the ground that never shifts.

There is a joy that has never come or gone. It is what you are. It has never cost you anything, because you've never been apart from it.

Like seeing that the sky and the color blue are never two.
