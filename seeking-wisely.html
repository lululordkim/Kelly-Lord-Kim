Seeking Wisely

On mistaking correlation for causation in nondual teaching.

For some, rest comes without needing to understand how instructions work or why seeking continues. For others — and I was one — the analytical mind keeps churning, trying to understand how instruction and seeking operate. This piece is for those minds.

Nondual traditions often describe awakening as recognition rather than production — something noticed when the assumption of separation loses its authority. When awakening is understood this way, instruction begins to function differently than it first appears.

For example, instructions like "stop seeking" or "be in the now" accurately describe what's observed when recognition occurs. They correlate with awakening. But when offered to seekers as causal methods, they reverse the logic: now you're trying to produce awakening by producing what merely correlates with it. This is the confusion of correlation with causation — like trying to cure a fever by cooling the thermometer.

The bind becomes: you're told to do something when there's nothing to be done, to get something that cannot be gotten.

Reports vs. Instructions

Some statements in nondual teaching are reports — descriptions of what's noticed when the sense of separation isn't organizing experience. Others are investigative prompts — attempts to reveal beliefs or redirect attention. The confusion arises when a report of what correlates with recognition gets treated as a method that causes it.

Consider "be in the now." As a report, it describes experience when seeking has quieted. But heard as a causal instruction, it implies someone outside the present who must enter it and maintain it. This turns "now" into an achievement and time into a problem.

As an investigative prompt, however — "Am I present, here and now?" — it functions differently. It doesn't try to produce a correlation; it investigates present experience.

How Seeking Perpetuates Itself

When "stop seeking" is heard as a causal instruction, a familiar pattern appears. Seeking may briefly relax, producing a sense of relief. But soon the activity returns in a subtler form: monitoring whether seeking has stopped, comparing present experience to an earlier ease, assessing progress. Now seeking turns into an attempt to produce the correlation — non-seeking — in hopes it will cause awakening.

But if awakening is not caused, and if there's no separate self that can travel to an enlightened destination, how do you seek wisely?

Seeking as acquisition is organized around lack and time — a future-oriented effort to obtain what's missing. Investigation is different: it examines what's being experienced right now, asking what is true and what thought is adding.

When this is seen clearly, investigation and rest are both relieved of causal responsibility. The pressure to produce something dissolves. What remains is openness to whatever appears, exactly as it appears.
