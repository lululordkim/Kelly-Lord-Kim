Practicing
the Presence

A translation and nondual commentary on Brother Lawrence's letters and conversations.

Brother Lawrence was a seventeenth-century Carmelite monk who spent most of his life working in a kitchen. He never wrote a book. What survives are letters and transcribed conversations, gathered after his death under the title The Practice of the Presence of God.

What he describes is not a technique. It is a recognition. The presence is not achieved. It is noticed — in the kitchen, at the stove, in the middle of ordinary work — as the ground that was always already here. This is the fifth of the first principles: practice is recognition, not construction.

His method, if it can be called that, is inseparable from the via positiva — not emptying the mind of content, but recognizing awareness as the ground of every moment, including the most mundane. The dishes, the stove, the noise of the kitchen — all of it the presence of God, already here.

This translation and commentary reads Brother Lawrence through a nondual lens, drawing on Dzogchen and the Christian contemplative tradition simultaneously. The project is published under Not-Two Publishing.

As The Constant Within notes: John leaning on the heart of Christ, Brother Lawrence washing dishes in prayer, Eckhart listening for God beyond thought — these are not separate paths. They are the same recognition, wearing different clothes.
