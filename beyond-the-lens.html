Beyond the Lens
of "Me"

There's nothing to eliminate.

The harder we try to eliminate a false self, the more solid it becomes. Effort makes it feel real. What looks like spiritual progress can distract us from what's already present and delay our recognition of what we are.

We often hear: you are already what you seek. But without direct recognition, that simple truth can become a project — an attempt to transcend the false self to reach the true one. That innocent move creates the very split we hope to dissolve.

What's actually happening is simpler: the separate person we've been conditioned to feel ourselves to be and the freedom that wisdom teachings point to are not two. They are the same awareness, sometimes wide open, sometimes seen through a narrowed lens.

Right now, as these words are read, what knows them? It isn't a little person inside who owns awareness. What we call "me" is a learned idea. In reality, there is one knowing — awareness itself — taking the shape of thoughts, sensations, and the experience of being a person.

How the Lens Gets Narrowed

From the beginning, we're trained to narrow our view — an aperture turned down until awareness seems to live behind the eyes of a separate someone. This doesn't change what we are; it simply filters how our experience appears through the lens of division.

Life then gets read as subject versus object: "anxious me" versus "danger out there," "worrier" versus "imagined futures," "lonely me" versus "others." This is separation bias operating in real time.

What This Understanding Changes

There is no self to destroy, no ego to transcend, no person that must vanish before awareness can appear. What seemed like a limited self was always this immediate knowing, read through a subject–object lens.

So the question isn't, "How do I get rid of a false me?" but, "How does the lens of division soften?"

We can stop when the sense of separation shows up. We don't need to elaborate, fix, or analyze. Let it rest where it appears — in awareness itself. Notice how contraction relaxes on its own when it isn't being managed.

Then anger reveals the love that protects. Anxiety opens into the care that tries to keep us safe. What looked like enemies are movements of the same awareness, seen through a narrowed aperture.

The Simple Truth

Sometimes awareness appears as a personal lens; sometimes it rests as itself without overlay. Awareness doesn't shrink or expand. Reality doesn't change. Only the lens shifts.

The lens moves; what looks through it does not.
