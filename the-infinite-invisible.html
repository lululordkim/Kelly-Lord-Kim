The Infinite Invisible

When emptiness reveals fullness.

This reflection was shaped by Joel Goldsmith's talk "Law to Grace" (1956), in which he spoke of a tree without coconuts. No fruit. No sign of supply. Just the appearance of material emptiness. His invitation was not to fix the appearance, but to recognize that true supply is never in the fruit — it is in the presence from which all things appear.

There are seasons when nothing seems to grow. No insight. No relief. No provision. The branches look bare. Other trees bear fruit. You reach. You wait. You whisper the words: God is my supply. I live by grace.

Still, the longing. Still, the unmet need.

Joel Goldsmith spoke of that tree — without coconuts. No fruit. No proof. Someone might hang borrowed coconuts on the branches — a check, a kind word, a temporary lift. And it helps, for a while. But beneath it, you know that what comes from outside fades.

The longing for supply is supply itself, seeking to know its own nature.

The thought "I am barren" arises, but can you locate it? Where does that sense of lacking begin or end?

Even before any answer, there is what Goldsmith called "the infinite invisible" — that presence in which even the feeling of lack appears. The seeking itself unfolds inside something that doesn't move.

You are not the one who lacks. You are the presence in which both lack and fullness appear — the invisible awareness that neither increases with gain nor decreases with loss.

The tree, barren or not, and the fruit are one movement of love. This presence doesn't measure by outcomes. It doesn't wait for proof. It simply is the space where thoughts of barrenness rise and dissolve, where seeking unfolds and completes itself.

Even the sincere seeker, so earnest in their longing, appears within this unmoving stillness.

You are not the tree waiting for fruit. You are what appears as tree, as fruit, as the seasons of fullness and the seasons of apparent want.

The one who longs and the longed-for supply are the same love, playing at forgetting and remembering itself.

Sometimes love reveals itself not through abundance but through the appearance of absence — through empty branches and unanswered prayers that quietly invite you to see what was never missing.

These are not punishments. They are thresholds. Not calls to effort, but to stillness.

There is never anyone separate standing beneath the branches, waiting for fruit. There is never anything missing to begin with.

You are the infinite invisible, appearing as every longing and every fulfillment.
