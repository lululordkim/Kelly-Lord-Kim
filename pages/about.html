What I've Seen in
25 Years

Even the most accomplished people suffer. That's where the work begins.

For over twenty-five years, I've worked one-on-one with senior leaders at companies like Airbnb, Amazon, Anthropic, Apple, Coinbase, HP, LinkedIn, Meta, Reddit, Sony, Stripe, Twitch, and more. Many of these coaching relationships spanned several years — some lasting as long as five to twenty years — totaling thousands of hours of deep one-on-one work.

Through these relationships, from managers to CEOs, I began to see patterns beneath even the most polished résumés and high-performance vocabulary: even the most accomplished people suffer. Despite the titles, the credentials, and often even spiritual depth, many described a subtle but persistent sense of not being enough.

In executive coaching circles, this is often framed as "limiting beliefs," "imposter syndrome," or "nervous system dysregulation." These frameworks can be useful, but in my experience, they often remain incomplete if the deeper root goes unrecognized.

That root — as I've come to see it, and as many wisdom traditions also suggest — is the illusion of separation: the belief that we are cut off from one another and our essential nature. I coined the term separation bias to bridge this ancient insight with contemporary language.

What changed everything for me was studying Dzogchen. Gradually, all my coaching certifications, training programs, and years of experience fell into place. When I stopped seeing myself, my clients, or our challenges as separate from the intelligence of life itself, I was able to simply coach from a place of comfort, ease, and grounded positivity.

My coaching conversations became just that — conversations. Clients often came in feeling overwhelmed by problems, and 45 minutes later they felt so much more at peace. Not because we'd solved everything, but because we were simply taking the next indicated step.

Sky and the Color Blue

The title of this site comes from a traditional metaphor in the Dzogchen lineage, where awareness and its expressions are said to be inseparable — like the sky and the color blue. What we're ultimately seeking is already present, revealed in the recognition of our utter indivisibility from pure awareness.

This isn't just a poetic idea. It reshapes how we live, work, and relate to ourselves and others. It's also the ground of my coaching: working from inseparability rather than separation.

I am based in Sebastopol, California. I work with clients globally.
