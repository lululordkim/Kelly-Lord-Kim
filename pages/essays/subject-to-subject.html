Subject to Subject

On remembering what sees.

There's a general understanding moving through nearly every moment: I am the subject. The one who looks. The one who moves through people, places, tasks, thoughts, and feelings. The one to whom the world appears.

That much is unshakable. What shifts is how this subject is experienced — whether seen through the bias of separation or free of it.

When separation bias is active, the subject feels like "me," a personal center inside a body, looking out at everything else. Life becomes a kind of orbit. All is weighed by how it touches me. Even spirituality. Even the longing to be free.

We look outward and name what we see: beautiful or dangerous, nourishing or threatening. We look inward and do the same: this thought is helpful, that one a problem; this emotion is spiritual, that one too much.

It is still the same awareness doing the seeing — but its view is narrowed, tightened around a personal "me," interpreting and improving itself and its world.

Sit quietly, and it becomes possible to notice: everything that appears — sound, sensation, thought, memory, identity — comes and goes. Yet the subject remains.

The mind says, I am aware of these things. That is true. But the "I" in this sentence is not the small center we've been taught to believe. It is the unchanging awareness in which even the sense of "me" appears.

When the bias softens, the subject is no longer located. The seeing is open. Seer and seen are not two. And yet, the subject is still here — not a separate person looking out, but the looking itself.

Separation dissolves, not by removing or improving upon or swapping out a subject, but by seeing that what we are was never confined to that "me."

What remains is the subject unqualified by "me." The I AM that was never personal. The seeing that needs no objects to prove itself, because it already includes them.

And in its very openness, this subject is not barren. In the absence of distortion, it is lived as peace, as clarity, as love itself. Not love as an action, but love as the radiance of being-consciousness, inseparable from what it is.

You are not the "me" watching thoughts and experiences — you are the awareness in which both the "me" and everything it observes appear.

And from here, nothing is separate. Not even the forgetting. Not even the remembering.
