The Constant Within

A Christian mystical look at separation bias.

Notice the ceaseless movement of life within and around you. Thoughts arise — judgments, memories, plans — and fade without command. Feelings unfold — joy, weariness, longing — and pass like weather through the sky. Even the body shifts — breath, heartbeat, pain — each sensation here for a moment, then gone.

Everything changes.

And yet, through it all, there is something that does not.

What is it that remains unmoved?

To notice the movement of a stream, you have to be standing on the bank. If you were only the current itself, you could not see it flow. In the same way, the very fact that change is witnessed reveals that you are not the movement, but the stillness in which it appears.

Beneath every change is the same living awareness — the Life in which all change occurs.

In the Christian mystical tradition, this is the Christ-light within you, as you, the uncreated knowing through which God beholds God. This Life does not come and go with experience; it was here before thought, before memory, before the forming of a world.

You cannot find this constancy as an object, because you are it. The light by which you know yesterday's joy and today's pain is the same Light that is, "I AM."

When you know yourself not as the passing content of experience but as the awareness in which all appears and is known, you begin to see the subtlest veil of all — the thought of separation. The thought that says, I am apart. I am here, and God is there.

But even this thought is not sin in the moral sense; it is simply a forgetting — a contraction of vision. For how could what lives in God be apart from God? You can think you are separate, or feel you are separate, but you can never be separate.

Let this remembering become your refuge. As the sages taught, when loneliness or fear arises, do not strive to banish them. Let them rest in the Presence that holds all things. You do not need the storm to pass before you can know peace.

The feeling says, "You are alone." The knowing whispers, "I am with you always."

And as belief withdraws from the lie of separation, the illusion begins to dissolve. Not through battle, but through rest. In stillness, grace does what effort cannot.

You are safe to rest here. This is the rock beneath all movement. Those who have walked before you knew this rest — John leaning on the heart of Christ, Brother Lawrence washing dishes in prayer, Eckhart listening for God beyond thought.

In this rest, the peace of God reveals itself as love. Love reveals itself as wisdom. And wisdom shines as excellence in all that you do — not yours, but His, living through you as you.
