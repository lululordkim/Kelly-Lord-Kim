Unbroken Presence

The recognition that ends seeking.

We speak as if presence were a state — something we enter and leave. As if we drift in and out of being.

But can you ever step outside of what you are?

Walking a path, lost in thought, you later say, I wasn't present. Yet the knowing of that "absence" — what is it, if not presence itself?

The awareness that sees distraction was never distracted. The fog is known by what it seems to obscure.

You can forget to notice, but you can't fall outside noticing.

"Be more present." The phrase hides the error. Presence isn't reached. It's what every reaching appears in. There is no one who leaves awareness. No outside to return from. Presence isn't an act but the ground of every act.

Distraction is not the loss of presence. It's presence appearing as thought, as wandering, as the moment that says, I forgot.

You don't return to presence. You notice who or what remains. The effort to be more present is the last movement of separation — the imagined self trying to become what it already is.

Nothing to reach. Nothing to hold.

Awareness never slept. It is this — the light by which even forgetting is known.
