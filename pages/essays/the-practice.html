The Practice

Beyond investigation to inseparability.

There comes a point in practice when the familiar questions rise: Am I doing this right? Should I be more aware? Am I present enough?

This voice imagines awareness as something to acquire, presence as a state to manage. It treats what is already whole as if it were lacking.

But awareness does not begin with effort. It is prior to effort, the silent knowing in which even the questioning voice is heard. It is what makes these words present now.

When a thought appears, you do not make yourself aware of it. The thought is simply known. When a sensation arises, there is no learning to notice it. Noticing is already the case.

This knowing does not improve with discipline, nor does it diminish with neglect. It is not altered by success or failure, storm or calm. It simply is. In itself, it is bare, without quality — empty knowing. And yet when it is no longer veiled by separation, it shines as love, as clarity, as the wisdom of wholeness.

If there is a practice, it is only this: to recognize what was never absent. To see that the seeker is already the sought.

Practice is not a movement toward an object called awareness, but a resting in the ground from which all arises. The mystics called it the Godhead, or the intelligence of life: still in itself, yet appearing as love in expression.

This recognition is never confined to a special moment. It may be seen in the appearance of a thought, in the passing of a feeling, in sound, in sight, in the pulse of sensation — all moving through the same indivisible knowing.

There is nothing to achieve, nothing to perfect. The awareness present now — closer than breath, already whole — is what every path points toward. Not a goal ahead, but the ground of being itself.

You are this awareness itself. The search and the searcher are not two. Even forgetting is enfolded in what never forgets.
