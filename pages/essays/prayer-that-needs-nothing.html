Prayer That Needs Nothing

On recognizing what has already been given.

This piece was inspired by Joel Goldsmith's approach to prayer — his reminder that prayer is never a beseeching, because God is already all-loving and all-giving. Nothing needs to be asked for; only recognized.

For a long time, prayer felt like reaching. Stretching outward with words toward a God I imagined was somewhere else, holding what I thought I needed.

Beneath that reaching was the quiet assumption that made the reaching feel necessary: the belief that I was apart from the very Presence I was trying to touch. This is where fear begins — in the belief that help could be absent. This is separation bias at its most intimate.

What Fear Is

When prayer comes from that place, fear feels inevitable. Hope too — the thin kind, barely covering the trembling underneath. Separation makes it feel like everything depends on us. Our effort. Our worthiness. Our spiritual performance.

Even prayer becomes another form of work — pleading, bargaining, trying to receive what was never withheld.

What if the Presence we long for was never missing?

The Exhaustion of Asking

There's a tiredness that comes from years of reaching — asking for clarity, for healing, for peace. Even when the words are gentle, the posture of asking carries tension, as if we must stretch further, speak louder, try harder to be heard. And all the while, what we were asking for was already closer than breath.

Real prayer has nothing to do with outcomes. It needs no words, no technique, no spiritual achievement. It is stillness recognizing itself. Love, resting in love.

Sometimes it appears as a breath. Sometimes as the space between thoughts. Sometimes as the simple knowing that nothing needs fixing.

Making Space

When we stop praying for things, space appears. In the mind, and in the whole being. And what we thought we needed often arrives — because we allowed.

Even fear belongs here. Fear is love appearing as protection — the movement of care inside the stillness that holds everything. Even the trembling that makes us pray is not apart from what we pray to.

What Remains

The Presence was never absent. The love was never missing. The separation was never real.

What we call prayer is simply love, recognizing itself as everything that seems to seek it. What remains has never needed anything to be different.

The reaching, the finding, the resting — all movements of the same wholeness, loving itself as everything it appears to be.
