What Remains?

Experience comes and goes — pain, loss, heartbreak. Yet the knowing of experience remains.

Experience comes and goes — pain, loss, heartbreak. Yet the knowing of experience remains.

Heartbreak and loss do not happen outside what knows them. What seems to break is never outside of what knows the breaking.

This isn't consolation. It points to something in direct experience. Even now, whatever is present is known, and there is something here that knows it.

Just as pain arrives, wanting arrives — wanting something to happen, wanting what has yet to come. Both appear in the same knowing.

Suffering begins when either becomes a conclusion: this should not be happening; this should be happening; something has gone wrong; I am alone in this. The conclusion is an interpretation added on top, and is itself an experience. When the conclusion is believed, it solidifies. When attention rests on its being known, it dissolves — without any pain or desire needing to vanish first.

You are not the experience. You are what every difficulty and desire appears in — the same knowing that does not come and go.

Forgetting and remembering both occur in that knowing.
